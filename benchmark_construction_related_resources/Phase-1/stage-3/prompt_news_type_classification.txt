[Rules for News Type Classification]
1. Choose exactly one news type.
    1.1 Current
        Use this label for time-sensitive reporting focused on recent or ongoing events.
    1.2 Feature
        Use this label for longer-form narrative, human-interest, lifestyle, or cultural reporting that is not primarily driven by immediate timeliness.
    1.3 Special_Report
        Use this label for in-depth or investigative reporting based on extensive evidence and deep examination of a specific subject.
    1.4 Attribution_QA
        Use this label for interview or Q&A-style content presented primarily through explicit attribution or direct quoted exchanges.
    1.5 Opinion
        Use this label for authorial viewpoint, editorial commentary, persuasion, advocacy, or normative judgment.
    1.6 Background_Analysis
        Use this label for explainers or analytical articles that synthesize background, context, causes, implications, or interpretations.
    1.7 Profile_Biography
        Use this label for person-centered reporting about an individual's life, background, experiences, or activities.
    1.8 Review
        Use this label for critical or evaluative content about creative works, services, products, performances, or consumer experiences.
    1.9 Satire
        Use this label for humor, irony, parody, or exaggeration that should not be interpreted as literal factual reporting.
    1.10 Unspecified
        Use this fallback label for noisy, mixed, ambiguous, or out-of-scope items that cannot be reliably assigned to another news type.

2. Classification rule.
    2.1 Select the single best-fitting category based on the article's primary communicative function and textual form.
    2.2 If the article combines multiple styles, choose the category that best describes the dominant format and purpose.
    2.3 Use Unspecified only when the article is too noisy, mixed, ambiguous, or out of scope to classify reliably.
