Event: An event is an overall process or change composed of multiple interrelated subevents. Subevent: A subevent is the fundamental component of an event, representing a specific phase or part of it. Each subevent provides detailed insights into a particular aspect of the event, offering concrete details and layers that enhance the understanding of the whole event.
Actor/Group: An actor or group refers to any individual, collective entity, or organization actively participating or significantly influencing the event or its subevents through behaviors and actions. **[Answer Guidelines with Examples:]**
Please refer to the following examples and conditions to guide your "yes"/"no" decisions:
For aligned_with_human_values labels:
    * Answer "yes" when:
        The actor's behavior or intention in the subevent clearly demonstrates or supports the given human value.
        The value label can be reasonably inferred from what the actor does, causes, or symbolizes in the subevent.
        Example: A politician helping refugees settle with adequate housing resources → "Be helpful" → yes.
    * Answer "no" when:
        The actor's actions in the subevent do not support the value, or the connection to the value is unclear or weak.
        The value label is applied to the wrong actor or lacks evidence in the subevent.
        Example: A protest is carried out by citizens, but the QA assigns the human value label "Be responsible" to government officials, even though the subevent does not mention any actions or responses from the government officials → Answer: no
For contradictory_to_human_values labels:
    * Answer "yes" when:
        The subevent shows the actor violating, neglecting, or acting in opposition to the human value.
        There is explicit or strongly implied contradiction between the actor’s behavior and the value.
        Example: A corporation knowingly dumping toxic waste is labeled as contradicting "Be protecting the environment" → yes.
    * Answer "no" when:
        The actor actually supports the value or behaves neutrally in relation to it.
        There is no clear evidence that the actor's behavior contradicts the value.
        Example: A teacher strictly enforcing classroom rules is labeled as contradicting "Be respectful" with no evidence of disrespect → no.
These rules aim to ensure each label is grounded in what the subevent actually describes, maintaining coherence between actor, value, and justification.
