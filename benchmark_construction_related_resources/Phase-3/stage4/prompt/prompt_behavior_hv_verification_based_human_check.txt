[Task Description:]
You are given a factual news article with its extracted subevents and behavior-based temporal events (aggregated behavior sequences). For each atomic subevent, human value QA verification has already been performed. Thus, each subevent-level human value label has a binary verification result:
    * answer = "correct": the subevent-level value label is correct (the actor’s behavior truly reflects or contradicts this value).
    * answer = "incorrect": the subevent-level value label is incorrect (the actor’s behavior does not meaningfully reflect or contradict this value).
Based on these verified subevent-level labels, you must now evaluate candidate human value labels for behavior-based temporal events. Each behavior-based temporal event is an aggregated unit constructed from multiple behaviors extracted from one or more related subevents, combined into a temporal sequence of behaviors. Your task is to verify the appropriateness of each candidate human value label at the behavior-based temporal subevent level, using:
    * The list of related atomic subevents.
    * The verified human value labels of those subevents (including both correct and incorrect labels).
Each value label is categorized as either:
    * aligned_with_human_values: the actor's behavior is thought to reflect or uphold a given human value.
    * contradictory_to_human_values: the actor's behavior is thought to violate or go against a given human value.

**[Key Concepts:]**
Human Value: Human values are broadly defined as the principles, beliefs, or standards of behavior considered important by individuals and societies, though their definition may vary across disciplines such as philosophy, psychology, sociology, and ethics. These values often guide decisions, actions, and interactions in life. In this task, to avoid cultural bias and knowledge bias encoded in the adopted model, we use a widely-recognized human value hierarchy. The human value categories are hierarchically organized, ranging from broad motivational goals (e.g., openness to change, self-enhancement) to specific values (e.g., creativity, freedom).
Event: An event is an overall process or change composed of multiple interrelated subevents. Each event is typically triggered by a specific cause and involves key elements such as participants, time, location, and objects. The subevents occur either sequentially or concurrently, collectively forming the overall narrative and background of the event.
Subevent: A subevent is the fundamental component of an event, representing a specific phase or part of it. Each subevent provides detailed insights into a particular aspect of the event, offering concrete details and layers that enhance the understanding of the whole event.
Actor/Group: An actor or group refers to any individual, collective entity, or organization actively participating or significantly influencing the event or its subevents through behaviors and actions. Actors/groups include but are not limited to people, institutions, governments, companies, social groups, or any identifiable entities whose actions, decisions, or expressed values impact or shape the progression of the event.
Aligned with Human Values: The content supports, promotes, or aligns with a specific human value.
Contradictory to Human Values: The content contradicts, opposes, or violates a specific human value.

This whole task comprises the following subtask:
[Subtask: Behavior-based Human Value Label Verification (Using Subevent-level Evidence)]
* Goal:
    * For each behavior-based temporal subevent and each candidate human value label (for a given actor), evaluate whether the label is appropriate at the behavior-level. You must make a three-way judgment:
        * "yes" — if the value label is clearly and sufficiently supported by the evidence and is correctly assigned to the actor at the behavior-based temporal subevent level..
        * "no" — if the value label is clearly contradicted by the evidence, assigned to the wrong actor, or strongly unsupported by the overall evidence
        * "cannot_judge" — if the available evidence is too limited, too vague, or highly conflicting, so that you cannot reliably decide "yes" or "no".

[Instructions:]
1. Use verified subevent-level labels as evidence.
    * Treat subevent-level labels with answer = "correct" as reliable positive evidence that the corresponding actor, in that subevent, truly aligns with or contradicts the given value.
    * Treat subevent-level labels with answer = "incorrect" as negative evidence: they indicate that this actor–value–polarity combination is not supported by that subevent.
    * Only use a subevent-level label as evidence for a candidate if the actor in that label is the same as the candidate actor; if the actors differ, that subevent-level label must be ignored for this candidate.
2. Decision procedure (you MUST follow this order):**
    * Answer "yes" if:
        * The actor’s behavior across the related subevents, as supported by correct subevent-level labels, clearly reflects or contradicts the candidate human value at the aggregated, behavior-based level.
        * The value can be reasonably inferred from multiple relevant subevents whose verified labels indicate consistent behavior regarding this value.
        * The evidence is strong and direct; do not answer "yes" based only on vague impressions or one very weak hint.

    * Answer "no" if:
        * The actor’s behavior at the behavior-based level clearly does not reflect or contradict the value.
        * The candidate label is only backed by subevent-level labels that were verified as "no", or correct subevent-level labels suggest an opposite or incompatible value.
        * The value label is assigned to the wrong actor or is strongly not supported by the overall evidence.
        * There is strong reason to believe the label is inappropriate for this actor at this behavior-based temporal subevent.

    * **Answer "cannot_judge" if:**
        * The evidence about this actor and this value is very limited, partial, or only indirectly implied.
        * The related subevents do not provide enough concrete information to decide whether the actor truly aligns with or contradicts the value.
        * The available signals are mixed or conflicting, and you cannot confidently choose "yes" or "no" even after careful reading.

3. Do not re-judge subevent-level labels.
    * You must trust the given verification results (answer: "correct"/"incorrect") at the subevent level.
    * Do not change or reinterpret subevent-level answers; instead, use them as fixed evidence when reasoning at the behavior-based level.
4. Be specific and evidence-based.
    * In your explanation, refer to:
        * The actor’s key actions or decisions across the related subevents.
        * Whether "correct" subevent-level labels consistently indicate alignment or contradiction with the candidate value.
        * Any contradictions, mismatches, or lack of support from subevent-level evidence.
